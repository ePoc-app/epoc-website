Recorded video sequences that can include images, sound, and animations. This content can be used to provide demonstrations, presentations, interviews, or explanations on a specific topic.

## Automatic compression

To ensure smooth playback on most devices and keep your ePocs lightweight, the editor automatically compresses videos when you import them.

Videos are converted to MP4 with the H.264 codec at 480p resolution (AAC audio codec), the same settings recommended for [manual encoding](#manual-encoding). A compressed video is often several times smaller than the original: your ePoc downloads faster for learners and is easier to host or upload to a learning platform ([HTML export](/en/guide/user/export-and-publish/html) and [SCORM export](/en/guide/user/export-and-publish/scorm)).

::steps{level=5}
##### Add a video
Import the video as usual (video content, teaser, etc). The `.mp4`, `.mov`, `.avi`, `.mkv`, `.webm`, `.flv` and `.wmv` formats are compressed.
##### Wait for the compression
An **Import in progress...** window shows the compression progress. It takes more or less time depending on the length of the video and the power of your computer.
##### Check the result
The compressed video is added to the ePoc with a `-min` suffix (for example `my-video-min.mp4`). Your original file is not modified.
::

::alert{icon="lucide:info"}
If the compression fails, the original video is imported as is.
::

### Disable automatic compression

Compression is enabled by default. You can disable it if your videos are already optimized or if you need a resolution higher than 480p:

::steps{level=5}
##### Click **Settings** at the top right of the editor
##### Turn off the **Enable auto compression for videos** option
::

The setting applies to videos imported afterwards: videos already in the ePoc are neither recompressed nor restored. To replace a video, import it again.

## Manual encoding

If you have disabled automatic compression, we recommend encoding your videos yourself before importing them: MP4 format, H.264 codec, 480p resolution. This format is sufficient for smooth, good quality playback on most devices.

### Encoding with ffmpeg

To encode one or more videos with [ffmpeg](https://www.ffmpeg.org/), use the following commands:

::alert{to="https://www.ffmpeg.org/download.html" target="_blank" icon="lucide:link"}
Download FFmpeg
::

```sh
# Encoding a video where "video.mp4" is the original video and "video-min.mp4" is the compressed video
ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -vf "scale=-2:480" -c:v libx264 -crf 23 -c:a aac -strict -2 "video-min.mp4"

# Encoding all videos in a folder
for file in $(find . -name "*.mp4" | sort -z); do
    ffmpeg -i "${file}" -vf "scale=-2:480" -c:v libx264 -crf 23 -c:a aac -strict -2 "${file%.*}-min.mp4"
done
```

### Encoding with Handbrake

If you prefer using a graphical software, we recommend [HandBrake](https://handbrake.fr). To do this, follow these steps:

::alert{to="https://handbrake.fr/downloads.php" target="_blank" icon="lucide:link"}
Download HandBrake
::

::steps{level=5}
##### Open HandBrake and load your video file
##### Select the "General > Fast 480p30" preset
##### Make sure the output format is MP4 and the video codec is H.264.
##### Choose an output location and name for the video
##### Click "Start" to begin encoding.
::

These recommendations ensure maximum compatibility and optimal quality for playback on various devices.
